Summer 2027 · about two weeks · three stops

Greece, three stops

A city, a real Greek island town, and a resort to finish. Three places over about two weeks, so nobody is packing every other day, with boat days for whoever wants them and a beach or a town for whoever doesn't.

Stop 1The cityAthens 3–4 nights

The Acropolis, the rooftops, the food markets, dinner at nine. Arrive off the overnight flight and lose the jet lag somewhere worth losing it.

Stop 2The real GreeceOne island town, still to choose 4–5 nights

A working island with an actual town: restaurants, a bakery, a harbour with fishing boats, all on foot. Enough visitors that a big American group is normal, few enough that a coffee still costs what a coffee costs. Good beaches nearby, not the whole point.

Stop 3The resortRhodes, Kos or Crete 5–6 nights

Somewhere to stop moving. A resort or villa cluster that takes the whole group, with great views, a great pool, easy sea access and good food on site. A town at the door is a bonus, not a requirement; boats and day trips leaving from nearby are.

Stop 1 · the city · 3–4 nights

Athens

Athens is the first stop: the nonstop from Newark lands in the morning, so the first day is a long, slow one (check in, a swim, a walk, an early dinner) and then two or three full days before the islands. The city runs late and warm in summer, so dinner at nine on a rooftop is normal, and some of the best things to do with kids happen after dark.

Athens is the one stop where the teenagers have a city: Plaka and Monastiraki for shopping and ice cream, Psyrri for the evening, the Lycabettus funicular for the sunset photo, and the Riviera beach clubs at Glyfada (Balux) on a hot afternoon. After-dark options are the point here; the beaches are not.

Where to sleepElectra Metropolis, on the Koukaki side of the centre with indoor and outdoor pools and family rooms, is the leading hotel candidate; a big group would take a block of rooms there. The alternatives are two or three apartments in the same building in Plaka or Koukaki, or one of the Plaka villa-class rentals that sleep a large group under one roof.
Stop 2 · the real Greece · 4–5 nights · one of five

The island town

Five candidates, presented as equals. Every one is a real working island with a town you can walk, a harbour with fishing boats in it, and food worth the trip, and there is always souvlaki, grilled chicken and fries for anyone who has had enough octopus. They differ in how quiet they are, how easy the beach is, whether the whole group can stay together, and how the hop onward works. Nothing has been ruled out.

Why not Mykonos, Santorini, Paros, Milos or IosThey are the islands everyone has heard of, and that is the problem: in summer they run on cruise crowds and Instagram, with menus and hotel rates priced for a different trip. Mykonos might as well be Miami Beach. The five below have the same light and the same sea, real towns, better food, and prices that still make sense.
Stop 2 · option 1 of 5

Karpathos

Stop 2 · option 1 of 5

Karpathos

Deep, traditional, a little far from its beaches

Who it fits. For travellers who want authenticity, will rent a car, and would treasure stumbling into a village festival. The trade-off: the pool-and-beach base and the food story are an hour apart. Onward: a 45-minute Olympic Air flight to Rhodes (Kos via Rhodes), or Sky Express to Sitia in eastern Crete.

A long, narrow, mountainous island in the southern Dodecanese, a short flight from Rhodes but temperamentally on a different planet. Mountain villages still keep communal customs the mainland lost fifty years ago, quiet beaches line a wild east coast, and nothing is performing for anybody.

Stop 2 · option 2 of 5

Leros

Small and quiet, with one great dinner

Who it fits. For travellers who value quiet, iconic scenery, and one splurge dinner. The trade-off: outside Mylos the food is solid rather than memorable, and there is no big-beach infrastructure. Onward: the Sky Express island-hop flight to Kos or Rhodes (with stops) or the morning catamaran, about 3 hours 50.

A small, sheltered island north of Kos, best known for something unusual in Greece: a preserved 1930s Italian modernist port town. Very few Americans visit. Good for travellers who value quiet over animation and would trade a big beach scene for a great walk and a great dinner.

Kalymnos

Stop 2 · option 3 of 5

Kalymnos

A working port with the best boats

Who it fits. For travellers who rank fresh fish, a working port and their own rental boat above whitewashed prettiness. The trade-off: the beach base and the food centre are 30 minutes apart, and Pothia is a busy port town, not a postcard village. Onward: the Sky Express island-hop flight to Kos or Rhodes (with stops) or a 3-hour catamaran.

Rugged rather than cute: bare limestone mountains dropping straight into the sea, a big amphitheatre of a harbour town, and the sunset behind Telendos from the Massouri side, which is one of the best views on any island on this page. What it is not is whitewashed-Cycladic. Known for sponge diving and world-class rock climbing; less known for having the largest small-boat fishing fleet in the eastern Mediterranean, which is why food-serious travellers go. Pothia, the main port, is a real working harbour with some grit at the quay.

Stop 2 · option 4 of 5

Astypalea

Remote on purpose
Asiomou · Wikimedia Commons
Stop 2 · option 4 of 5

Astypalea

Remote on purpose

Who it fits. For travellers who want quiet and a remote feel above variety or convenience, in a place that is used to summer visitors without being built for them. The trade-off: fewer restaurants and the smallest hotel stock of the five. Onward: the Sky Express island-hop flight to Kos or Rhodes (with stops) or a 4-hour catamaran.

A butterfly-shaped island at the boundary of the Cyclades and the Dodecanese, so far off the tour routes it belongs to neither. A hilltop white-cube village crowned by a medieval castle, a tiny working port below, no cruise ships. Quiet is not empty: Greeks and Italians have holidayed here for decades, the Chora bars and tavernas fill up in July, and there are proper hotels and suites at Livadi. Americans are simply rare, which is a different thing from unwelcome.

Stop 2 · option 5 of 5

Naxos

Big and fairly touristed, for good reason

Who it fits. For travellers who want producer tours, a cooking class, and full-service infrastructure. The trade-off: sharing all of it with a crowd. Onward: a 55-minute Aegean nonstop to Rhodes ran in summer 2026 (once or twice a week), 2027 not yet published; Sky Express flies to Heraklion; the fallback is a change in Athens.

The largest island in the Cyclades and the most agriculturally serious of the five, with long shallow sandy beaches, mountain villages, Venetian towers, and food producers you can actually visit. Also, by a wide margin, the most touristed and busiest of the five, and in early summer that shows.

Stop 3 · the resort · 5–6 nights · one of three

The finish

Three ways to finish, presented as equals: the same job (a place that takes the whole group, with pools, sea and food, and boats leaving from nearby) done by three different islands. Two different models have worked for this group before and they are not the same thing. The house: one big villa with a pool over the water, everyone under one roof, cooking and drinks our own, cheaper per family, and it only works if everyone commits to the same week. The resort: family suites booked side by side, half board or dinner credits so nobody plans meals, clubs for the kids and the teenagers, a real town a short walk or a water taxi away, and families can come for three nights or six. Which model the finish takes is half of the anchor question below.

Stop 3 · option 1 of 3

Rhodes

The big island: castles, sheltered beaches, resort coast
dronepicr · Wikimedia Commons
Stop 3 · option 1 of 3

Rhodes

The big island: castles, sheltered beaches, resort coast

Who it fits. For a resort finish with the most to do around it: the medieval Old Town and Lindos are the trip's big history, and the sheltered east coast between Kallithea and Lindos has the deepest stock of family resorts and villa clusters with pools on organised beaches. The trade-off: it is the busiest of the three finishes, and the resort strip is a resort strip. Getting there: a 45-minute flight from Karpathos, the Sky Express island line from Leros, Kalymnos and Astypalea, a two-hour catamaran from Kos; home is a same-day one-stop to Newark via Athens.

Rhodes is the big island at the far southeast of Greece, with a medieval walled city at one end and a cliff-top acropolis (an ancient hilltop citadel) at the other. The rule that decides everything here: the east coast is sheltered and sandy, the west coast is windy and pebbly, so all the family beaches are on the east side.

Where to baseThe natural base is the mid-island east coast around Kolymbia and Afandou: from there it is about thirty minutes to Rhodes Town, thirty to Lindos, and ten to Tsambika beach, which is the best balance for a stay that includes both the Old Town and Lindos. The far-south resorts are roughly an hour from the Old Town, a real cost on a short stay, and villa stays anywhere on this coast should be assumed to need a car.
Stop 3 · option 2 of 3

Kos

The resort island, done properly
Ввласенко · Wikimedia Commons
Stop 3 · option 2 of 3

Kos

The resort island, done properly

Who it fits. For a resort finish with the whole group under one roof: Ikos Aria at Kefalos puts pools, an organised sandy beach, kids' and teen clubs, a spa and a dozen restaurants at one address, ten minutes' walk from a real village. The trade-off: more package tourism than the small islands, the north coast catches the meltemi, and Kefalos is 40 minutes from Kos town. Getting there: a short flight from Athens, or from Karpathos, Leros, Kalymnos and Astypalea on the Sky Express island line; home via Athens.

Long, narrow and flat, with a busy resort coast in the north and a quieter, whitewashed end at Kefalos in the south. As a finish it does the resort job well: a real airport with several Athens flights a day, sandy organised beaches, two water parks, and the one resort on this list built for families at scale. The history is here too: Hippocrates was born on Kos, and the Asklepieion where he taught is a short ride from town.

Stop 3 · option 3 of 3

Crete

The biggest island, a real resort belt, and the most to see
dronepicr · Wikimedia Commons
Stop 3 · option 3 of 3

Crete

The biggest island, a real resort belt, and the most to see

Who it fits. For a resort finish with the strongest resorts and the most beyond the gates: the Elounda and Agios Nikolaos coast in the east is a cluster of villa-and-suite resorts on a sheltered gulf, with Spinalonga island a short boat hop away and Knossos an hour off. The trade-off: Crete is huge, so the west (Chania, Balos, Elafonisi) is a different trip from the east, and getting there from the small islands means a change in Athens or a Rhodes connection. Home is via Athens from Heraklion or Chania, several flights a day.

Greece's biggest island, big enough to have a city, mountains, and coasts that feel like different countries. For this trip the relevant part is the east: the Gulf of Mirabello around Elounda and Agios Nikolaos, calm water, views to the Venetian fortress on Spinalonga, and the densest run of luxury family resorts in Greece. Agios Nikolaos itself is a lakeside town with cafés and shops on foot. Chania's Venetian harbour, an hour and more to the west, is the other Crete, and probably a separate visit.

Where to baseElounda or Agios Nikolaos in the east for the resort brief; Chania in the west if the beaches and the old town matter more than the resort. Not both on a five-night stay.

What we pick together

Four things to settle, in this order, once the dates are set. The finish first, because it fixes the flight home and the resort. Then the island, because the choice depends on how it connects to that finish. Then how we stay on the island. Athens first or last can wait.

Which finish

Rhodes, Kos or Crete. Rhodes has the most around it and the verified one-stop flight home. Kos has the one resort built for a big family group and is the easiest hop from the small islands. Crete has the strongest resorts and the most to see, and the longest journey to get there.

Which island

Five live candidates above, presented as equals. Every one has a short flight from Athens, and every one gets to Rhodes or Kos by air (Karpathos nonstop; Leros, Kalymnos and Astypalea on Sky Express's island-hopping line with a stop or two; Naxos had a seasonal nonstop to Rhodes in 2026, not yet published for 2027). Crete is a change in Athens from most of them. Summer 2027 schedules publish over the winter.

House or suites on the island

The trip already has both models. The resort finish is where family suites work: meals handled, clubs for the kids, people can come for three nights or six. The island town is where one big house works: everyone under one roof and committed to the same dates, cooking and drinks our own, the best value. The live question is that island stop: one house for everyone, or suites and small villas a few doors apart so each family keeps its own dates? The group has done both and liked both.

Athens first?

Athens is first for now because the Newark nonstop lands there in the morning and the city is the easiest place to lose a jet-lag day. It is also the hottest stop, and late June is hot: the argument for three nights rather than four, for the Acropolis at 8 AM and the rooftops after dark, and for doing the city before the islands rather than after, when everyone is already tanned and tired. The case for last: the flight home leaves from Athens anyway, so ending there removes the same-day connection from the resort. Open.
